# Owner questions: football, cricket and the Australian winter code

Everything here was built without these answers and ships with a labelled default or an
empty slot. Nothing below is a measurement, and no article has a size chart yet: every
point of measure on these articles is **not set** until someone supplies a number.
Each answer is a change of a line or two of data.

## Australian winter code

1. Which code does the partner pick: AFL, rugby league, rugby union or netball? Today the
   article `winter-code-top` ("Team top, winter code") has one sport choice, "Code not chosen
   yet", and no code is guessed. Once picked, the code is added as a choice of the `winter-code`
   option, and a code-specific article may be split out (for example a netball dress, which
   is not a top).
2. Does the code need its own garments beyond a top (shorts, socks, a dress)? None are
   built.

## Socks

3. What is the sock factory's knitting programme (machine gauge, yarn, cushioning zones,
   heel and toe shape)? None was supplied, so socks are labelled "knitted, factory programme
   not supplied", have no pattern and no fabric details, and the pattern library shows
   "No programme yet".
4. How are sock sizes named: by shoe size ranges rather than S, M, L? Socks use the usual
   adult and youth size runs for now, which is probably wrong.
5. The seam entries on socks (toe closing, leg and foot join, top edge) are placeholders so
   the registry can describe every part. A knitting factory will say what really applies.
6. The sock drawing is a straight, symmetrical schematic with the foot in line with the leg,
   not the bent shape of a worn sock. Is that acceptable for a tech pack, or does the factory
   want the L-shaped flat?

## Goalkeeper shirt

7. Which options does a goalkeeper shirt need besides padded elbows (padded forearms, grip
   panels, a different neckline, short sleeves)? Padded elbows is an option only with a long
   sleeve, which is a proposed rule.
8. The padded elbows are described in the tech pack as a part ("Elbow padding") and are not
   drawn on the flat. Should they be drawn, and with what shape and size?

## Cricket

9. Cricket cap blocks: which shapes does the partner make (peaked club cap, baggy cap,
   wide-brim sun hat, helmet-style cap)? Only a peaked cap and a baggy cap are offered, and
   their shapes are Knitwright proportions, not a block. The cap has four points of measure
   (head opening width, crown height, peak length, peak width) that a hat maker should
   replace with their own.
10. Do cricket trousers need a belt loop, button fly, pleats or a pocket type other than
    side seam and zipped? They are built from the bottoms family and have none.
11. Is the coloured shirt a collared, buttoned shirt in the partner's range, and does it
    come with a long sleeve and a short sleeve? Both are offered, with a polo collar and
    buttons to start.

## General

12. Football and cricket training tops, shirts and shorts reuse the proportions of the team
    jersey, long sleeve tee, polo and shorts drawings (see `lib/flat/OWNER-QUESTIONS.md`).
    A football shirt is not drawn differently from a team jersey. What differs, if anything?
13. Starter size charts: shirts and shorts could reuse the existing starter charts for the team
    jersey and shorts, but nobody has said that a football shirt is cut like a team jersey.
    Which chart should each article start from?
14. Women's and youth cuts: all articles are offered for men, women and youth with the same
    options. Does the partner want different options or articles for any wearer?
