# 0038: Design from the user's image (W7d, phases A and B)

Status: Proposed
Date: 2026-10-14
Plan step: production outputs, W7d (`plans/production-outputs.md`)

## Context

A club hands over a mockup, a flat, a photo of a kit or a sketch and expects its design on the real pattern pieces. A picture cannot be turned into an exact copy: light, folds, angle and small lettering are lost or baked in. The owner's requirement is a close reading, shown beside the picture, with the limits stated, and nothing kept until the person accepts it. Phase A built the pipeline up to the artwork stack. Phase B adds the all-over pattern stage (W7b's `findRepeat` and W7c's generators), intake through W7b, stored pictures and one-click replace for redrawn marks.

## Decision

1. **A staged pipeline in `lib/design-import/`**, every stage returning a confidence and a plain note, every value `read-from-image`: reading (`read.ts`), outline (`outline.ts`), landmarks (`landmarks.ts`), fit (`fit.ts`), marks (`marks.ts`), colours (`colours.ts`), the pattern stage behind an interface (`pattern.ts`), then the W7a stack and a report (`assemble.ts`, `report.ts`). W7a's types, `PieceMapper` and asset reader are imported and not changed.
2. **Text in the picture is data.** The vision call goes through the existing text-model provider with the rule of `lib/intent/image-input.ts`. The reply is parsed by a strict schema (unknown fields drop the whole reply, text is stripped of control characters and cut to 80 characters), so a picture cannot make the reader do anything but report what it shows. Words that look like instructions (`looksLikeOverride`) are still read as printed text and noted to the person. A reply that obeyed the picture is not valid JSON of the shape and is refused.
3. **Classical geometry first.** Outline by corner colour distance, largest connected region, holes filled, traced and simplified. Landmarks from the mask: the neck dip, the neck's top edge, where the sleeve steepens, where a sleeve's underside meets the side (read along the fitted side line, so a photo's lean does not fool it) and the hem. The model's guesses fill only landmarks the picture did not give, at low certainty and listed as guessed. A landmark the person drags wins. The image model (fal's edit model, already in `lib/providers/fal.ts`) is used only when no outline is found, within a budget of 2 edits, on a picture padded to a square; its pixels are flagged generated.
4. **Fit through `PieceMapper`.** A flat or mockup is fitted by a least-squares affine map from picture to design millimetres; a worn photo by a homography from its landmarks first (affine with a note when fewer than four are found). The share of the outline that lands on a piece for the size is read through the mapper and reported. Until W1's pattern library and W3's mapper are on `main`, the template is the benchmark tee (W0 fixture, measured from the DXF) stood upright from its own landmarks, behind a `LandmarkTemplate` interface; the report says so. Only front and back bodies are fitted (decision 0034: body only); a sleeve or collar view is left empty and named.
5. **Honest about what is missing.** A view not in the picture is left empty and named, never invented. Marks the reading could not read are not added. A crest, sponsor or badge is a `graphic` layer flagged "redrawn from a picture, replace with your own file"; phase A does not store its crop (that is W7b's asset store), so the layer names an asset id that does not exist yet and the accept answer lists those layers.
6. **Nothing is saved until accept.** `design_imports` (migration `20261015060200`) holds the proposal, the stored reading (so redoing a fit costs no vision call), the report, the overlay, the brand, the rights statement time and the cost. `accepted_stack` is null until the person accepts all or some layers; a check constraint ties the status to it, and a trigger refuses any change to an accepted or discarded row. Server writes only; members read their brand's rows. Applying the accepted stack to a project's design is phase B, because it needs W7b's assets for the crops.
7. **Switch, budget and cost.** `DESIGN_IMPORT=on` turns the feature on; off or unset, every route answers 404 and no screen is shown (owner question P12). At most 3 vision calls and 2 image edits per import; a per-person daily cap (`LIMITS.designImport`, 10 a day) and a per-brand daily cap in the service (10 in 24 hours), both proposed low (P16). Vision cost is the chosen model's token price (`MODEL_CATALOG`, `IMAGE_INPUT_TOKENS`); the image edit price is a catalog entry (`IMAGE_EDIT_CATALOG`) read from fal's published price recorded in `docs/data-processing.md` (fal.ai was not reachable from the build container, so it is to be re-read). The first vision call uses the route's reservation; each later call and each edit reserves its own and gives it back on failure.
8. **The reading asks for a JSON object in plain text,** so a model the provider layer forces onto the assistant's reply tool cannot be used (`pickVisionModel` takes the first configured model with free tool choice, today Claude Sonnet 5.5). Letting the request name its own tool is a small additive change to `lib/providers/text-model.ts` and is left to a `contract` PR if the owner wants a cheaper model.
9. **Words.** The screen says "close reading of your picture". The words "exact", "matches your picture" and "identical" are never used (a test scans the report, the limits and the screen). The six accuracy limits are always on screen beside the picture and the drawing.

## Phase B: the all-over pattern stage

10. **Intake through W7b** (`intake.ts`). The uploaded file goes through `intakeArtwork`, the door every artwork uses: type from the first bytes, size and pixel limits, sRGB, and a vector mockup (SVG, or a PDF or Illustrator page) drawn to a picture. The reading and the maths use W7b's screen copy (re-encoded as a smaller JPEG if it is over a vision call's 5 MB); WebP, which artwork intake does not take, is made a PNG first. W7b's sentences about the file (colour profile, see-through parts) go into the report's notes. The panel still uploads through `/api/files`, so it offers image types only; a vector file in storage is read.
11. **Finding a repeat** (`match/stage.ts`). The flat is classified into the garment's own colours (a blend at an edge is not a colour: a small colour on the line between two stronger ones is dropped). In the biggest clear block of the garment, W7b's `findRepeat` gives the period along each axis. Along an axis the picture does not vary on (the length of a vertical stripe), there is no period and the repeat is shown for one axis only. The repeat in millimetres is its pixels times the flat's step, which the fit sets from the garment's width, and it is always shown in centimetres as "estimated, check it".
12. **Matching a generator** (`match/estimate.ts`, `match/search.ts`). Only styles whose settings can be read from a repeat are tried: polka dots (`kit-dots`: pitch, row step, dot width, half drop), stripe variations (width and angle, from both periods) and camo (one drawing, since its blobs are seeded and only a picture made with the same seed fits it). Each start is drawn once by the W7c generator over a frame a little bigger than the block, rasterised to colour numbers, and slid over the block pixel by pixel to find where the pattern starts, so sliding costs no new drawing. A block of four periods or more is scored in four windows, each finding its own start, so a slightly skewed fit of a photo is not counted against the pattern. The best start is tuned by nudging each setting up and down (8, 4, 2 and 1 percent), keeping what lowers the error. The error is the share of pixels whose colour differs from the picture's.
13. **Deciding.** At or under `ERROR_LIMIT` (15 %, a setting, not a measurement) the generator is proposed (a `pattern` layer: clean, scalable, recolourable) and the repeating picture is kept as `pattern-<view>-alt`, an `image-fill` at opacity 0 that is not drawn until chosen. Over it, the repeating picture is proposed, with the closest style and its error said in the report. With no repeat but a busy garment (at least 8 separate patches of colour) the view is kept as one full-panel `image-fill`; plain blocks of colour are not read as a pattern. The pattern and its alternative are one or the other: accepting everything leaves the alternative out, choosing it draws it, choosing both is refused.
14. **Budget** (`match/budget.ts`). At most 120 trial drawings and 120 million pixel comparisons, and 5 seconds, for each picture; the first two are counts, so the answer is the same on every machine, and the clock only stops the search early (the report says when it did). The limits are stated in every report that reads patterns.
15. **Pictures are stored as artwork.** The tile or panel is cut from the picture at the original's own sharpness (`cuts.ts`, a copy of up to 1600 px, with the marks repaired from the cloth), written as a PNG that states its resolution, and stored through W7b's `uploadArtwork` with the rights the person ticked at the import; the layer names the stored artwork. A PNG cannot state under 50 dpi, so the layer's `scale` makes up the difference and the picture prints at the size it was cut at. Crests, sponsors and badges are stored the same way (at most 8 a picture). The effective resolution is W7b's `dpiWarning` for the repeat's width, with a sentence saying the 150 is Knitwright's default and not the print house's. Pictures for layers that are not accepted, a discarded reading or a redone fit are removed, best effort.
16. **One-click replace** (`POST /api/design-import/[importId]/replace`). A redrawn crest is replaced by the person's own file, taken in through `/api/artwork` (the rights box, the print and screen copies); the route only points the layer at that artwork, which must be the brand's (the asset reader answers for one brand). The layer keeps its width and takes the file's proportions, its provenance becomes `user-edited`, and a redone fit keeps it replaced. A layer needs its own file while it is a `graphic` still `read-from-image`.

### Phase B evidence

Synthetic pictures are drawn by W7c's generators with settings written down in the test, so the truth is known; CI uses recorded replies. Measured on the synthetic tee sheet (1.5 mm to a picture pixel; front and back): polka dots with a pitch of 45 mm, rows 22.5 mm apart and 20 mm dots are read as `kit-dots` with the pitch 5.8 % off, row step 0.4 % to 1.3 % and dot width 1.5 % to 2.5 % off, half drop read as true; the repeat is 47.6 mm for a truth of 45 mm (5.7 %, from the garment's width as fitted). Diagonal stripes of 24 mm at 45 degrees are read as `stripe-variations` with the width 0.4 % off and the angle 45.5 degrees. A camo repeat of 90 mm is not matched by any style (about 44 % of pixels differ), so it is an `image-fill` tile of 91 mm, and the report says so; a camo with no repeat is a full-panel picture per view. No quality claim is made for real pictures: the golden set the owner owes (P13) is needed first. The photo case (a perspective, shaded input) reads the pattern through the same fit, so a skewed fit costs error, not the match.

## Alternatives (phase B)

- Match every style: most of them cannot be read from a repeat, and a setting made up to fit would be an invented measurement. Only the three that can be read are tried; the rest stay a repeating picture.
- One global start for the whole block: a fit of a photo that is a degree or two off skews the pattern across the block and counts against it. Four windows, each finding its own start, cost the same comparisons.
- Search the seed of camo: unbounded, and a camo from another source would never fit. It is a repeating picture.

## Evidence

Tests use synthetic pictures drawn from a known tee, with perspective for the photo case, and recorded replies; CI never makes a live call. Landmarks are within 6 px of the truth on a 400 px flat and within 8 px on a perspective, shaded photo-like input. No quality claim is made for real pictures: the golden set the owner owes (P13) is needed first.

## Alternatives

- Ask the model for landmarks and outlines: slower, dearer, and it would be the model's word for geometry we can measure.
- Always flatten with the image model: invents pixels, costs money on every picture.
- Save the proposal as the design and let the person edit it: the owner requires acceptance first.

## Consequences

W1 and W3 are still needed for the real template and mapper, a line in `lib/workspace/tools.ts` (W0's file) to mount `DesignImportPanel`, and the owner's golden set. `design-import` is now in the 90 % coverage thresholds of `vitest.config.mts`. W7b's index gained one additive export (`uploadArtwork`, `deleteArtwork`, `Actor`) for storing the pictures cut from a design. Both providers receive the user's picture; `docs/data-processing.md` and the privacy page need the paragraph sent on the PR (owner question P12).
