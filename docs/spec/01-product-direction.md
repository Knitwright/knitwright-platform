# 01 · Product direction

Version 2.1 · 25 Oct 2026 · replaces the 22 Oct 2026 product spec where they differ. 2.1 adds the founder's Studio journey: inspire, design, engineer, make, shoot, sell.

## What Knitwright is

**Knitwright is a sportswear factory network with software attached.** It makes custom sportswear in Sialkot, Pakistan, and the software exists to make that production easy to buy, hard to get wrong and hard to leave.

Two front doors share one backend:

| Surface | Who | What they do | Domain |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Knitwright Teamwear** (the beachhead) | Club kit managers, players, parents | Design a kit from a template or their crest, get committee approval, collect sizes, open a team store, pay, receive in about 4 weeks | `teamwear.knitwright.com`, club pages at `<club>.knitwright.com` |
| **Knitwright Studio** (the platform) | Founders launching sportswear brands, designers | Get live inspiration and suggestions, design on factory-approved base articles, learn why each rule exists, get a tech pack and production files, order a sample then a run, shoot campaign images, then publish to a store or order bulk | `studio.knitwright.com`, storefronts at `<brand>.knitwright.com` |

Behind both:
- **Ops console** (internal) turns paid orders into factory batches, QC records and shipments.
- **Factory job packet** (magic link, no account) lets a factory see its job, download files, update status and upload QC photos.

Teamwear comes first because its demand is countable (124,500+ FA teams, 43,000+ ECB teams, 3,300+ Football Australia clubs) and Sialkot already makes sublimated teamwear at 15–25 piece minimums. Teamwear volume proves the file pipeline, colour control and QC that brands will rely on later. Studio is the long-term platform; it is built on the same design object, catalogue and production backend.

## Decisions

These are settled. Do not reopen them in code; raise them in an issue.

1. **Knitwright is principal and merchant of record for every order.** It buys from the factory and sells to the club or brand. There is no open factory marketplace, no per-order commission on third-party factories, and no holding of customer money for onward payment ("escrow"). This avoids UK payment-services regulation and factories cutting Knitwright out.
2. **Launch production is Sialkot only**, through partner factories under contract. More factories are added later as Knitwright-managed capacity (still principal), not as marketplace listings.
3. **Launch markets: UK first, Australia second.** EU and US are closed at launch (US: about 42% duty on polyester knit tops and no de minimis; EU: per-item duty and fees, GSP+ renewal uncertain). The platform must store country rules so they can open later without schema changes.
4. **Two production classes:**
   - **Class A, sublimated on demand.** Sportswear-specific sublimated polyester articles (jerseys, shorts, training tops, compression, fightwear) from MOQ 1, produced in a contracted weekly print slot. Not leggings for fashion activewear; Printful already sells those for about $24–37.
   - **Class B, small runs on stock-colour fabric.** 50–300 pieces per style from a pooled fabric programme: 3–5 base fabrics in a fixed palette bought by Knitwright. No custom-dyed fabric at launch (about 1,000 m minimum per colour).
5. **Manufacturable by construction.** Every design is a base article with factory-approved parameter ranges. The editor can change the surface freely and the structure only within those ranges. Nothing generated by AI bypasses this.
6. **Factory-native files.** The production package ships 1:1 PDF and SVG/EPS panel layouts (which CorelDRAW opens) alongside DXF-AAMA, plus a points-of-measure table with tolerances, a colour spec with ΔE tolerance and a golden-sample reference. One sample round is budgeted per new base article per factory.
7. **Benchmarks, not copies.** The research tool compares functional specs and review complaints. It never clones a product, never reproduces logos, prints or names, and runs a design-around check. Product names containing "dupe" or a third-party brand are blocked.
8. **Revenue:**
   - Core: production margin.
   - Studio Pro subscription (about $29/month, to be confirmed): storefront custom domain, Knitwright branding removed, up to 15% off base prices, more media generations.
   - Club fundraising margin on team stores (paid to the club).
   - Media credits priced at cost plus margin, refunded as order credit when the design is ordered.
   - Not at launch: research credits, file-export fees, factory verification fees.
9. **Design is free.** Designing, versions, 3D views, benchmarks (fair-use limit), quotes and the tech-pack summary are free. Exporting the full production package to take elsewhere costs a one-off fee per design (about $49, to be confirmed), refunded as order credit if the design is produced by Knitwright.
10. **Inspiration is live, but it is only ever a reference.**
    - **Sources:** Knitwright's own library, the user's own Pinterest boards through the official API, licensed stock APIs (Unsplash, Pexels), link previews of products the user adds, AI mood images, and search buttons that open Behance and Pinterest in a new tab. Behance has no public API and neither site is scraped.
    - **What happens to an image:** it can become a *suggestion*: Knitwright's own parameters and pattern library, re-rendered and validated. It is never placed on a garment.
11. **Every rule explains itself.** Each validation check, option range and tech-pack value links to an entry in Knitwright's rulebook, a curated knowledge base written with the factory. The AI phrases and selects rulebook entries; it never invents a manufacturing fact.
12. **The Studio journey is fixed:** Inspire → Design → Engineer → Make → Launch content → Sell (see `02-features.md`). Each stage hands the same design object to the next.
13. **Design references (28 Oct 2026):**
    - **Fabra** for the design studio, 3D model and tech pack.
    - **Kittl** for mockups, marketing images, video, Flows and Brand Studio.
    - All templates are sportswear-only (`07-template-library.md`).
    - AI never re-draws the garment: mockups and scenes always start from the locked 3D render or golden-sample photo.

## Principles that settle feature arguments

1. **The order is the test.** Build what moves an order through the loop before anything that widens it.
2. **One design object everywhere.** Configurator, Studio, team store, storefront, tech pack, files and factory sheet all read the same versioned record.
3. **The texture is the print file.** The 3D model's UV layout equals the sublimation panel layout, so what the user sees is what is printed.
4. **AI proposes, the user decides, the system constrains.** AI output becomes parameters and layers, never pixels on the garment.
5. **Nothing reaches a factory that ops cannot produce.** A design is orderable only when it validates against a factory profile.
6. **Honest promises.** Delivery dates come from the next batch cut-off plus measured lead times. Renders sit beside a true-colour photo. AI imagery is labelled.

## Users and jobs

| Role | Job to be done | Surface | Success looks like |
|---|---|---|---|
| Club kit manager | Get a kit designed, approved by the committee, sized for 15–30 players, paid and delivered before the season | Teamwear, club workspace | Kit designed in 20 min; sizes collected without a spreadsheet; delivered in 4 weeks |
| Player or parent | Pick size, add name and number, pay | Team store link | 2-minute checkout on a phone, no account |
| Committee member or sponsor | See and approve the design | Committee link | Views, comments and approves without an account |
| Brand founder | Turn an idea into a product that sells, without a technical designer or a 1,000-piece MOQ | Studio, storefront | Benchmark → design → sample in 2 weeks → first run of 50–300 → live storefront |
| Designer | Build designs and templates for clubs and brands | Studio | Templates used in orders (royalties come in Phase 4) |
| Knitwright ops (Sialkot) | Batch weekly, send jobs, QC, pack, ship | Ops console | No manual artwork work per order; defects under 2% |
| Partner factory | Receive a job it can produce without questions | Job packet link | No back-and-forth; Class A in 5–8 working days, Class B in 15–25 |

- **Factory evidence:** sublimated jersey FOB $8.20–9.40 at 100 pieces; names and numbers $0.80–1.80; sample $25–80 (supplier self-claims, May 2026).
- **Competitors:**
  - NOKTO: €38.95–47.95 per jersey at 10–100+, names/numbers included.
  - Spized: €57.90–72.90 for 1 piece, €37.90–53.90 at 20.
- **Knitwright target club price, names/numbers included:**

  | Pieces | Price per jersey |
  |---|---|
  | 10–19 | £29 |
  | 20–49 | £25 |
  | 50+ | £22 |

  Free UK delivery on the batch. Single-piece reorder £39.
- **Gross margin floor:** 45% after freight, duty and payment fees. The price ladder is data in the factory profile, never hard-coded.

## Not in scope until later phases

- Open marketplace or directory of third-party factories.
- US and EU selling.
- Custom-dyed or custom-knit fabric.
- Free-form pattern drafting.
- Seamless knit.
- Fashion leggings and bras on nylon/elastane.
- A template marketplace with royalties.
- Weekly single-parcel made-to-order to consumers outside the UK and Australia.
- Cloth physics.
